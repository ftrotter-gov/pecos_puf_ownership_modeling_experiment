Caveats and Known Limitations

Read this before quoting any number from this report. The data is useful for finding and exploring ownership records. It is not an authoritative measure of market concentration, and several of its headline counts are known to be wrong in specific, documented ways.

1. Owners are only partly de-duplicated

CMS identifies an owner by enrollment ID, not by company, and one real company holds many. Left uncorrected, a corporate family appears as several separate "ultimate owners".

A data patch fixes the cases that are demonstrably the same entity — 116 duplicate owner IDs merged, each with a stated reason. HCA was the worst case:

Before the patch Orgs After
HCA INC 115
HCA HEALTHCARE INC 112
HEALTHTRUST INC - THE HOSPITAL COMPANY 111 → HCA HEALTHCARE INC — 117 orgs, 218 hospitals
HERCULES HOLDING II 104
FRISCO HOLDING II 103

89 hospitals had listed all five at once.

What is still wrong

Only families with clear evidence were merged, so smaller chains remain fragmented. Two deliberate exclusions:

  • Corporate form is never merged. TRIAD HEALTHCARE CORPORATION and TRIAD HEALTHCARE LLC stay separate — Inc and LLC are distinct legal entities, and merging them would assert a fact not in evidence.
  • Automatic clustering was rejected. Grouping owners that share ≥95% of their hospitals chains unrelated companies together through genuine joint ventures: at a 2-hospital threshold it fused Tenet, LifePoint, CommonSpirit, CHI and Texas Health Resources into one 113-member "company". Even at ≥10 shared hospitals it pulled Vanguard, BlackRock and Invesco into Encompass Health.

Roughly 2.3% of owner names remain exact duplicates under normalization, down from 3.45% before the patch.

2. Index funds rank as owners

Vanguard and BlackRock appear near the top of the table. They hold 5–10% indirect (role 35) stakes in publicly traded hospital chains — index-fund positions, correctly disclosed to CMS, but a shareholding rather than control.

They are flagged in the patch as passive investors and deliberately not merged or deleted: removing disclosed ownership would falsify the data.

Note also that "VANGUARD GROUP INC" (the index fund) and "VANGUARD HEALTH SYSTEMS" (a Tenet subsidiary) are different companies with similar names.

3. The joint-venture flag is approximate

is_JV means "this organization has more than one ultimate owner". Because owner de-duplication is incomplete, a wholly owned hospital can be flagged as a joint venture purely because its owner still appears more than once.

Treat it as "multiple ownership records", not "multiple owners".

4. Depth measures reporting, not corporate structure

Maximum ownership depth is 2, which understates real corporate hierarchies.

PECOS records a hospital's direct (role 34) and indirect (role 35) parties as a flat list, not as a parent-of-parent chain. Five levels of one corporate hierarchy arrive as five siblings at depth 1. So depth describes how CMS recorded the structure, not how deep it actually goes.

5. Hospitals only

This is built from the CMS Hospital Enrollments public-use file. Hospices, skilled nursing facilities, and home health agencies are published separately and are not included.

The counts therefore describe an owner's hospital footprint, not its total healthcare footprint. An owner with 300 nursing homes and 2 hospitals shows as 2.

6. Source data quality carries through

  • 1,106 organizations are listed as their own owner, and 1,108 pairs each appear to own the other. Both are artifacts of officer and managing-employee rows rather than real ownership, and both disappear once the data is restricted to organizational ownership roles. The resulting ownership graph is acyclic.
  • Direct-ownership percentages sum above 100% for about 2% of enrollments in the source itself.
  • About 1% of hospital CCNs are malformed (a valid 6-character CCN with a suffix appended). They are passed through untouched.
  • Ownership is as-reported. PECOS reflects what providers filed, with no independent verification, and the snapshot is a point in time.

7. What would make this authoritative

Not implemented, and each is a project:

  1. External entity resolution — SEC EDGAR CIK for public companies, IRS EIN / ProPublica for nonprofits. The only way to be right rather than merely self-consistent.
  2. Control vs. passive classification with a defensible ownership-share threshold, so index funds stop outranking operators.
  3. The other provider-type files, for a complete healthcare footprint.
